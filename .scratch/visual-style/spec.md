## Goal

Replace the throwaway `src/render/scene.ts` with a renderer that draws a District as an anime-flavoured miniature diorama: a small Japanese-style town on a floating slab, readable as a traffic puzzle first.
Vocabulary in root `GLOSSARY.md`.

The target is the user's "Anime Map Diorama" design.
Its proof on the real sim was the prototype at `/diorama.html` (`src/lookdev/diorama.ts`, deleted from `main` in #32), kept with the three rejected looks on the local branch `16-visual-style-prototype`.
Its screenshots, `.scratch/game/reference/diorama-*.png`, show the original design, whose tone the palette keeps.

Reference screenshots of the game, retaken in [#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148): `.scratch/visual-style/reference/morning.png`, `noon.png`, `afternoon.png`, `dusk.png`, `night.png`, a Jam up close in `close-noon.png`, `close-dusk.png` and `close-night.png`, and a Rebuild half-way, a new Tile's piece of slab assembling, in `rebuild.png`.
They show the #104 mock's twelve Tiles, laid one cell south-west of where the mock lays them, in a Free run (`?free&seed=3`), with its two Changes to Priority rules and its west Crossroads jammed.
Each Tile is a Zone: the starting Crossroads shops, then low-, medium- and high-density residential, offices, industrial, parks, green and farms in the order they are laid, and round again.

This overturns three look-dev (03) decisions: the perspective camera, static AO in place of shadows, and the baked distance-field ground.

## The rule

Readability beats style.
Roads, markings, cars and jam feedback must stay as legible at night, zoomed out and mid-Reveal as they are at noon.
Any effect that softens them is cut before any gameplay signal is.

## Rendering recipe

- Three.js on WebGL, standard soft-lit materials (roughness about 0.85), neutral tone mapping.
- One directional light with a 4096² shadow map, plus a hemisphere light.
  A shadow's edge is blurred by one texel, so it is crisp ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)).
  Blurred decals under every building and tree give the contact shadow, in violet (`rgb(52, 44, 150)`) like the shadows the light casts.
- No cel bands, no outlines, no ink, no painterly filter.
  The only post-processing allowed was one bloom pass for night lights, and only if it cost under 2 ms per frame.
  It was measured in [#29](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/29) at 6.2 ms and cut, so there is no post-processing.
- Everything is procedural geometry and canvas-drawn textures. No authored 3D models, no image files.
- Static scenery is merged into one mesh per material. Cars, walkers and the moving details are instanced.
- **Surface pattern** ([#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174), `src/render/pattern.ts`): the soft-lit material that walls, roofs and the slab share lays a pattern over its vertex colours from one array texture of layers 128 texels square, drawn on canvas and mipmapped.
  A layer's red shades the paint, its green is window glass and its blue a pale trim.
  A part wears a pattern through two vertex attributes, `pattern` (which, and for a wall its bays and storeys or for a roof its size) and its `uv` (where on it each vertex lies), set as the kit adds a box: each face of the box wears the pattern its side calls for, in whole bays across its width so that no window is cut at a corner.
  A pattern costs no triangles and no mesh; a build in which nothing wears one carries no `pattern` attribute.
  Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) a Block's pavement wears its paving joints with no attribute: the vertex shader picks out a face that looks up at the kerb's height in the pavement's colour `#f1ede5`, so every Tile is paved and only a Zone whose own parts wear a pattern carries one.
  The glass takes one of the kit's glass tints by day, picked window by window, and 70% of the windows light at night with the kit's own windows' glow: the lit mask, which lights back and end walls without a mesh.
  Since [#177](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/177) an office tower's curtain wall is a pattern of its own whose glass is the wall's paint, the tower's glass, in three tones a third of its height at a time and a tone a pane, and whose lit mask lights whole floors, as many as the tower lights (a share its `pattern` attribute carries), each floor across all four faces;
  a round tower wears it by its cylinder's own uv, and a dome or a pointed cap a roof's pavers by its own.
  An office roof's deck and an office plaza's paving are patterns too.
  Since [#178](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/178) a terraced house's walls wear plaster (a fine speckle and a line at each floor): on its front round the windows it is built with, and on its back and its ends a framed window 0.9 m wide in every bay of about 2.4 m, which lights as the lit mask does, its back's middle bay on the ground floor a door with a lit pane;
  an apartment block's walls the front's plaster, a line at each floor of 3.2 m.
  A pitched roof (`tile`, a gable or a hip) wears kawara: ribs 0.25 m across down the slope in rows 0.25 m deep along the eaves, laid in metres along the eaves and down from the ridge, under a cap 0.22 m darker along the ridge; its gable ends pale boards.
  An apartment block's roof wears sheets of roofing a metre wide between raised seams, and alleys, courts and a shrine's gravel setts 0.3 m square.
- **Light pools** ([#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174), `src/render/movers.ts`): warm decals on the ground under the lights, with no light source, as the contact shadows are decals.
  One instanced mesh for the whole City, each pool riding the brick of the light it lies under and lit as the lamps are, hidden by day.
  A pool adds its light to the ground and takes the place of some of the moonlight there (60% at its middle, fading to its edge as its light does): added alone to the bright blue night, a warm light comes out white.
  Below the lamps' glow at dusk, as they come on before it and go out at dawn, a pool fades as a whole, so that it never darkens the ground.
- **Block-zoom props** ([#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175), the kit's `blockZoomProps`): the small props a Zone builds (pot plants, bicycles, crates, menu boards, goods in a window, stools, gas cylinders, an AC unit on a back wall) are painted on `blockZoom`, the soft-lit material drawn apart from `solid` with its patches, so each build has one more mesh.
  It is whole from the camera's zoom 3.2 inward (Tile zoom is about 4), drops a growing share of its pixels in a screen-space dither as the camera leaves Tile zoom, as the clouds fade in, and is not drawn at all from zoom 2.2 outward, where most Tiles are on screen.
  Its shadow fades with it, cast through the same dither, and a Tile assembling in a Rebuild, a Rezoning or a Stage-up shows its Block-zoom props as the zoom has them.
  A Block-zoom prop casts no contact shadow.
  Only this material's programs and the depth material its shadow is cast with carry the dither's discard.
  Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) the street's furniture is Block-zoom too: guard rails, convex mirrors, planters, bollards and benches, which the spec of [#173](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/173) has seen from Tile zoom inward;
  the dev switch `?furniture` draws it at every distance on `solid`, with its contact shadows, as [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) did round a shops Tile, for a comparison.
  What lies flat on the pavement and the plate (the kerbstone line, tactile strips, tree pits, drain grates, manholes and patches) is drawn at every distance.
- **Lit paint** ([#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175)): a sign, an awning or an arcade lights on the kit's bulb tinted to its colour, as #153's amber lanterns do: its colour by day and its glow at dusk, at a share of a lamp's strength low enough to keep its colour (`SHOPFRONT.lit` in `src/render/kit.ts`).

The style comes from shape, palette and proportion:

- **Shape:** every box is rounded and bevelled; houses are a wall box with a glass band under a gable roof; office towers are curtain walls of glass with no band per floor; trees are clusters of spheres (round, sakura) or stacked cones (pine).
- **Palette:** gently oversaturated, with pastel walls and towers, brick / teal slate / teal / tan / violet / persimmon roofs, fresh greens, pink sakura, a pale grey road and an off-white pavement.
  Every colour is under Palette below.
- **Proportion:** lanes about 7 m wide and Blocks roughly 40–60 m across, so a car reads as a toy and each building as an object.
  This was the single biggest difference between the first port (a 360 m roundabout District, rejected as "not the same") and the accepted one.

## Palette

Set in [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105) at the strength the user picked in [#104](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/104), "a little", the gentlest of three.
The user's reference for the tone is the original Anime Map Diorama design, which is the earlier palette under the morning light.
Saturation goes into each colour: there is no filter and no post-processing.
The road plate (`#c3c7ce`), the pavement (`#f1ede5`) and the white road paint stay pale and near-neutral, so cars, paint and Tool aids read against them.

**Reserved colours.** Two colours belong to the game's signals:

- Tool blue `#1f7aff`: the Tools in the world and the route Inspector.
  Since [#107](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/107) that covers the halo around a hovered or selected Junction, the Connections and the tube being drawn, the round markers at each Arm's line, and every lane end.
  The lane ends are circles where Connections start and rings where they end, both on white rims, which lift the blue off the moonlit road plate at night.
- Hot red-orange `#ff4a1f`: the anger marks, the speech bubbles' text, and the Tile in the hand when its drop is refused.
  That Tile takes 60% of the colour, and the tint glows (emissive 0.4) so it keeps its colour at dusk and at night.
  Since [#110](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/110) it also fills the Jam markers: a disc on a white rim over each Junction with a Jam, carrying the anger mark in white, with a tail that points at the Junction.

Within 30° of hue (OKLCH) of either colour, no scenery or car goes over 65% of that colour's chroma.
That rule is why the park's deck is wood rather than shrine vermilion, why the cars are turquoise and raspberry rather than blue and coral, and why the roofs are brick, teal slate and persimmon rather than terracotta, slate and salmon.

A clear cyan noon, an orange and pink dusk, and a deep blue night that stays bright.
The day blends between these and the morning, the late afternoon and the handovers between sun and moon, whose values are in `day.ts`.

Since [#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148) the light is coloured.
A shadow shows the fill alone, so the fill's colour is the shadow's: the [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105) fill moved 70% of the way to a blue-violet at every time of day (half way at noon), and its ground bounce 30%, so walls stay warm.
The sun is about 15–50% stronger and warmer in the morning, the afternoon and at dusk.
Two rules set the values:

- **No road in shadow is darker than one in [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105)'s dusk shadow**, the darkest the game had and still read.
  Dusk's fill is raised until its shadow matches that, and noon's and the afternoon's are lowered to it, which gives the navy shadows by day.
  The handovers stay brighter than dusk.
- **Whites in the sun stay white at noon:** a white surface lit by noon's sun and fill is neutral within 1% on each channel.
  That is why noon's fill moves only half way to the blue-violet and noon's sun is not warmed.

## Camera

Orthographic.
Left drag pans, right drag rotates and the wheel zooms toward the pointer, as set by [#15](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/15) and built in [#109](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/109); the camera buttons are in `.scratch/ui-ux/spec.md`.
Tilt is clamped between roughly 24° and 72° above the horizon.
Default view looks from the south-east at about 41° tilt with the whole slab in frame.

## The slab

- A District is drawn as a finite slab floating in open sky: soil, sand and grass layers, then a road-coloured plate.
  A soft shadow sits in the air beneath it.
  The sky is a three-stop gradient behind a transparent canvas.
- **Fog has no visual.** Unrevealed parts of a District are simply not there.
  Fog stays in the glossary as the rule that fogged Junctions do not exist in the sim.
- Each Level authors one or more rectangles in the District file: the pieces of slab its Reveal adds (harder Levels can add more).
  The slab is every piece Revealed so far.
- A Reveal only ever adds slab, next to the slab already there.
  A Level's Blocks are cut within its own pieces and keep clear of their outline, so a seam between two Levels' pieces is road plate and no later Reveal changes an earlier Level's Blocks.
  A Level's new Roads stay on its own pieces.
  (Settled with the user after the first Reveal playtest, 2026-11-14.)
- Roads run to the slab edge and stop. Cars grow in at a Gateway and shrink out at one. No tunnels, ramps or bridges off the slab.

## Roads and Blocks

- The road is the plate. A **Block** is a raised slab (about 1.2 m, bevelled) standing on it, coloured as pavement.
- Block outlines are cut from the lane geometry, not authored: a distance field over Road lanes at full width, the mouth of each Junction, and a car's width along every turning Connection (which rounds the corners), contoured with marching squares.
  The same field gives pavement centre lines for walkers and inset outlines for lawns.
  This handles a roundabout and a street grid with the same code.
  The ground bake from look-dev is retired, and its kerb-wave artefact with it.
- The prototype computes the field by brute force (1.3 s for nine Junctions).
  The real renderer needs a spatial grid over lane segments, and only computes the newly Revealed pieces.
- First version edges: the pavement is the rim of the Block, and a roundabout island is a raised slab with a tree.
  Bike bands, medians and splitter islands are cut.
  A Block under 12 m wide, such as the strip a seam leaves beside a Road, has no room for lots and carries a bike lane instead.
  The `bikeBand`, `sidewalk` and `median` Road attributes stay unread.
- **Paint:** a dashed centre line per Road, lane dividers where a direction has more than one lane, and zebra stripes at Junction mouths (decoration until pedestrians become a mechanic).
- **Priority** is shown in the world as road paint (give-way triangles on a yielding Arm) and is re-read from the sim on every Change.
  No in-world signs.
  Signal lamps are designed when the Signal Tool ([#7](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/7)) passes its gate test.

## Block fill

- **Authored:** landmarks and special Blocks (a tower plaza, a park with a pond, a shrine on a hill, a river with a bridge). They live in the District file.
- **Seeded:** every other Block is built as its Tile's Zone ([#130](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/130), [#136](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/136), `src/render/zones/`, one module a Zone), from the seed:
  - Low-density residential: houses on lawns behind low block walls, with their gardens and garden trees, built to the Finish ([#179](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/179), below). See [zones/low-density.md](zones/low-density.md).
  - Medium-density residential: terraced rows of narrow houses that share walls, or apartment buildings round a court (an O, a U, an L or a bar), built to the Finish ([#178](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/178), below). See [zones/medium-density.md](zones/medium-density.md).
    The seed picks one of the two for each Tile.
  - High-density residential: pale towers with windows lit one by one, a stack of balconies, a water tank and a roof garden, and a playground or a garden at their foot. See [zones/high-density.md](zones/high-density.md).
  - Offices: glass towers lit floor by floor at night, built to the Finish ([#177](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/177), below). See [zones/offices.md](zones/offices.md).
  - Shops: rows of low shops with coloured ground floors, each front a shop of its own with its signs and awning, with vending machines, nobori banners, paper lanterns and parked bicycles before the fronts that face a Road, a scene on each Block, and the Kerbside round them finished ([#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175), below). See [zones/shops.md](zones/shops.md).
  - Industrial: wide, low sheds with sawtooth roofs and roller doors, a chimney now and then, and a loading yard before the doors. See [zones/industrial.md](zones/industrial.md).
  - Parks: a park with a pond, or on a small Block, one time in three, a plaza with a fountain, built to the Finish ([#180](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/180)): a mown lawn, gravel paths, an azalea border, the pond's rocks, reeds, island and bridge, a shrine, a tea house or a bandstand, stone lanterns, and swan boats, ducks, koi and by night fireflies; a plaza paved in rings, with a clock tower or a bandstand, a kiosk, beds and pigeons. See [zones/parks.md](zones/parks.md).
  - Green: a meadow with groves and flower beds, or allotments. See [zones/green.md](zones/green.md).
  - Farms: fields of crop rows, and on some Blocks a farmhouse and a barn with a silo. See [zones/farms.md](zones/farms.md).
  - An unzoned Tile: grass with a few bushes.

  Where a Block lies seeds its choices and nothing more, so a Zone builds alike anywhere.
  The odd lot is left as a tree, and lots that would overhang a rounded corner are dropped.
  A coral tower landmark stands on an offices or a high-density residential Tile where the seed puts one.
- **Stages** ([#138](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/138), [#141](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/141)): every Zone builds at its Tile's Stage.
  The look above is Stage 2; Stage 1 is a humbler version of it and Stage 3 goes beyond it.
  Every lot draws from its own random stream, so each Stage builds the same building, better, and never another kind.
  Trees in yards, courts, parks, plazas and meadows grow with the Stage, to 55%, 100% and 112% of their Stage 2 size.
  Stage 1 to 3 in each Zone:
  - Each Zone's Stages 1 to 3 are in its file (Seeded, above).
  - A Tile that reaches a Stage changes lot by lot over about 3 s, in a random order.
    Each new building drops in as a Rebuild's bricks do while trees pop in, and the old one stands until the new one lands on it, then vanishes in the dust, so a lot is never bare.
    Confetti ends it.
- **The Stage Inspector** ([#139](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/139)) tints the ground of each zoned Tile, below a hand's height above the pavement, in the gold of a Stage's stars: `#fbeeb4`, `#f2c12e` and `#d29600` for Stages 1 to 3, in even steps of lightness (OKLCH 0.95, 0.83, 0.71) and all more than 45° of hue from the hot red-orange.
  Half the tinted ground's light is the tint itself, unlit, so it reads at dusk and at night.
  A Tile held back by a Jam is striped diagonally with a neutral grey `#7a7670`, 3 m stripes 6 m apart.
  It fades in and out over 0.2 s.
- Lamps stand at Block corners.
  Street trees line the avenues the seed picks, whatever the Zone, and what a Zone builds keeps clear of them: buildings, ponds, crop rows and allotment plots alike.
- **Utility poles** ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148), `src/render/poles.ts`) line one side of every Road: the south side of a Road running east–west and the east side of one running north–south, so a line meets the next Tile's on the same side.
  They are 9 m tall, 0.45 m in from the kerb, with a crossbar across the road carrying two wires that sag about 6 cm for each metre of span, and a transformer drum on every other pole.
  - They stand six to a cell's width, half a spacing (8.3 m) in from each edge, whatever the Tiles around, so the spacing runs on unbroken across a seam and a seam's wire always finds its poles.
    On an avenue they stand between the street trees instead.
  - A line carries on across its Junction where its Road runs straight on, though not across a Roundabout, whose island and Ring stay clear.
    On a Curve both lines take the outside of the bend and turn the corner on a corner pole standing on the kerb of the bend; where that puts a line on the other side from the next Tile's, the wire across the seam crosses the Road.
    A T-junction's stem joins the line across it on a corner pole, where one can stand.
  - Poles move up to 3 m along the Road to keep clear of a lamp, or are left out.
    Standing just in from the kerb, with their crossbars set toward the road, poles and wires clear what every Zone builds, which keeps at least 0.6 m back from the kerb, and the Zones' fill is unchanged.
  - A wire across a seam belongs to neither Tile: all of them are strung again whenever a Tile lands or an Undo takes one off.
- **Fallen petals** lie round every sakura, as many to the square metre whatever its size, out to seven times its size, on the Block only and, on a raised lawn, well in from its edge.
  They are drawn from where the tree stands, so no Block's fill changes.
- Each Zone's own details and finish are in its file (Seeded, above).
- **The Kerbside round every Tile** ([#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) round a finished Tile, `src/render/kerbside.ts`; on every Tile whatever its Zone, unzoned too, since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176)), each drawn from where it lies.
  Every Block's pavement wears the paving joints of #174's pattern, which the scenery material's shader lays wherever a face looks up at the kerb's height in the pavement's colour, so that no build carries a pattern attribute for it (one on every build cost parks and green 0.6 ms at City zoom); and every Block's kerb, a roundabout's island's and a narrow Block's bike lane's too, its kerbstone line.
  Every Block but a roundabout's island takes the rest of the street furniture, an authored park's or plaza's and a bike lane's too, which a Tile City never builds.
  A Block's other furniture keeps off what its Zone built near its edge, read back from what the Zone's fill laid: each part that rises 6 cm over the pavement within 4 m of the edge, laid point by point on a grid of half-metre cells, low ground (a lawn, soil, a bed) where it lies and anything taller kept half a metre further off, and the loop of each mover that works the ground, as wide as it is.
  Its planters, bollards and benches keep off the ground the Zone claimed as well, read from the kit's claims rather than a list of the Zone's own ([#218](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/218)): 1.1 m off what each piece stands on, and off the bays and door steps.
  A shop claims the pavement before its front out to the kerb.
  It keeps off the utility poles too: a rail, a grate or a planter never stands at a pole's foot.
  Rails, mirrors, planters, bollards and benches are Block-zoom props (Rendering recipe, above).
  The user chose this on 2026-11-18 from a sheet of shops with the whole City in frame, the furniture hidden and shown: it changes the shops reference of [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175), which drew them at every distance.
  Round each Block:
  - A kerbstone line 0.36 m wide along every Block's edge, just in from its bevel;
    a yellow tactile pad 4 m by 0.6 m where each zebra meets the kerb, with a guide strip 1.6 m in from it;
    a guard rail round each corner, 7.5 m either way of the point nearest the Junction, opening at the crossings and keeping off the lamp, the poles and what the Zone built there;
    a convex mirror on a pole facing the Junction at one corner in three, where no lamp, pole or building crowds it;
    tree pits round the street trees;
    drain grates on the kerb line of the straight runs, one every 9 m;
    and wherever 10 m of pavement runs bare of the Zone's lots, lamps, trees and the corners' furniture, a planter, a pair of bollards or a bench, in turn.
  - On the road plate, along the middle of each Road lane and 12 m clear of its ends, where the zebras and give-way paint lie: a manhole or a patch of newer tarmac every 26 m or so, within 6% of the plate's lightness.
    Each Tile lays only the marks wholly on its own cell, so that a Road between two Tiles is marked along both halves alike and none spills onto the next Tile.
    No new paint.
  - On the Tile's utility poles, every third carries a street light: an arm reaching 2.1 m over the road at 6.4 m with a lamp's head, lit at dusk, and a pool on the road under it.
- **The comparison of a packed Tile** ([#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175)): the dev switch `?deeper` lays the shops' rows 20% deeper (`?deeper=1.4`, 40%), leaving less yard behind them, for the user to set beside the open Tile as built.
  Lanes, Roads, the City and the sim are unchanged, so the Block itself cannot grow: a Block's edge is the lanes' edge.
- **Moving details** ([#149](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/149), `src/render/movers.ts`), the tractors first, are drawn instanced, one mesh for every mover of a kind, and placed every frame, outside the merged static scenery:
  - A mover works a loop that its Block's fill lays out, a stretch at a time: for a tractor, a stretch is a row.
    It sets out at the start of the working day, from 0.02 to 0.5 of the day's clock, works as many stretches as fit in 80% of the day, going round its loop as often as that takes, and stops at the end of its last one, a tractor a metre short of the row's end, until the next morning.
    Where it is follows from the work done, a clock that runs only through the working day and while the world runs, so it stops while the game is paused and carries on from where it was when a Swap, a Rezoning or a Stage-up builds it again.
  - Since [#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150) a mover may wait at each stop before it sets out on the next stretch, drive a stretch in reverse, facing the way it came, as a truck backs into its bay and a forklift out of a door, and take a paint of its own, which its model's white parts show.
  - A chimney's smoke ([#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150)) rides the chimney's brick and goes with it in a Stage-up.
    Its puffs are one instanced mesh of soft spheres, each faded by its own share of the opacity.
  - Since [#152](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/152) a mover may fly, rising off its path between stops and easing down onto the next, and beat its wings, which turn about its body in the vertex shader and cast no shadow.
    Each mover's bob and wingbeat keep a phase of their own, from where its loop starts, so none jumps when movers elsewhere come or go.
  - Since [#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155) things may hang and sway in the wind, laundry on a pole and futons over a railing, each riding its building's brick as a chimney's smoke does: one instanced mesh per kind, swung about the line it hangs from, washing about 0.2 rad either way and futons 0.04, leaning out on the wind and gusting, each on its own phase.
    Each is hung out at its own moment early in the working day and taken in at its own moment late in it, so none hangs out at night, and it stays still while the game is paused.
    They cast no shadow, a thousand at a time.
    Beacons ride their building's brick too, one instanced mesh of bulbs, each flashing on its own beat.
  - Since [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154) a mover may work a wall, its loop drawn on the wall's face and the mover facing out of it, as a gondola does; hang from an arm by two ropes that pay out as it goes down; and work whole laps of its loop a day, so that it ends each day at the stop it set out from.
  - Flags ([#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)), and any other kind of thing that flies from a pole, are one instanced mesh per kind for the whole City, each in a colour of its own and riding its pole's brick, so that no build gains a mesh for them.
    Each flies downwind, on the wind that leans the chimneys' smoke, with a wave running out along it from the pole in the vertex shader, on a phase of its own; its shadow flutters with it.
    It is raised as the working day starts and lowered to 3 m and furled as it ends.
    The waves stop while the world is paused.
    A nobori banner ([#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)) is a kind of its own, a tall narrow cloth hung from a rod across its pole's top: it faces the way its rod is turned, not downwind, stays at the top of its pole as it is furled, and its band of lettering keeps its own colour, as a swaying thing's unpainted parts do.
  - Since [#177](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/177) a mover may be away at one of its stops: unseen while it waits there, shrinking over the last 3 m of its loop to it and growing over the first 3 m from it, as a food truck is off down the Road until it turns in over the kerb.
    A food truck drives at 2 m/s.
  - Since [#178](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/178) a mover may be a delivery scooter, its rider aboard and a box on its carrier, driving at 2.6 m/s with a light bob, away off down the Road between its calls as a food truck is, as many laps a day as fit.
  - Since [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) a mover may be a shopper, walking at 1.15 m/s with a light bob, and a plume may be steam: puffs every 0.5 s that swell from 0.3 m to 1.15 m over 2.2 s, at 70% of smoke's opacity and a third of its drift on the wind.
  - Since [#157](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/157) a swaying thing may stretch its model along its line and down from it.
    The low-density gardens' laundry is a kind of its own, `cloth`: one piece pegged to a pole, a metre square stretched to the piece's width and drop, swinging as the towers' washing does, so that each piece on a pole is an instance in its own size and colour.
  - Each mover rides a brick of its own, which has no shapes.
    It pops in with the Rebuild, Rezoning or Stage-up that brings it, once the fields it works have landed, leaves at once with a Tile an Undo takes off, and stands until the old Tile's road plate is covered in a Swap.
    In a Stage-up a mover both Stages have stays where it is.
- **Every Zone's own details** ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)): each Tile type carries small details of its own, and some of them move.
  Each Zone's are in a ticket of their own, with the rules they share: they grow with the Stage, ignore Jams, follow the time of day, and are drawn a little oversized where they move or matter, while scatter on the ground stays true to size.
  Farms went first ([#149](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/149), above), since their tractors build the machinery for every moving detail;
  then industrial ([#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150), above), green ([#152](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/152), above), high-density residential ([#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155), above), medium-density residential ([#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156), above), offices ([#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154), above), parks ([#151](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/151)), shops ([#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)), low-density residential ([#157](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/157)) and a wilder verge on unzoned Tiles ([#158](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/158)).
- Districts are themed (city, countryside, harbor). The first is a city.

## Vehicles and jam feedback

- Cars only, in mixed colours.
  Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) the sim's one car type is drawn in five skins, all of its length, 4.3 m, and oversized across as the car always was, so that every skin reads as traffic alike: a stubby hatchback (the car as it was, on wheels), a tall kei car, a saloon taxi with its roof lamp, a box van and a kei truck with a flat bed.
  Each has four wheels, a band of dark glass that is its windscreen at the front, and head and tail lamps, pale and deep red by day and lit at dusk.
  Which skin a car wears and its paint are drawn from its id, so a car keeps both all its trip.
  Each skin is one instanced mesh, and its lights a second, unlit; the sim, its vehicle type and its length do not change.
  A bus skin arrives when the sim has a longer vehicle type.
- **Jam emotion is the main jam signal in the world.** A car that has been stuck for a few real seconds starts to shake and gets an anger mark; stuck longer, it steams and may pop a speech bubble.
  Marks go to the longest-stuck cars first.
  Bubble text is flavour only and never carries information.
- Clicking a car lights its remaining route as a glowing blue line above the road.
  Tool overlays (the Connection editor) use the same language: blue tubes and round markers.

## Day cycle

- A full day (morning, noon, dusk, night) loops on its own clock, a few minutes per day.
  It is cosmetic: it does not touch Demand, Levels or the sim, and the player cannot control it in the first version.
- One light is the sun by day and the moon by night; it goes dark for a moment at each handover so the jump across the sky is never seen.
  The hemisphere fill is raised through each handover, so roads are no darker there than at dusk.
- Night is a bright moonlit blue, not dark.
  Windows, lamps and car head and tail lights glow through emissive materials only; there are no per-object light sources.
  Beacons ([#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155), [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)) are emissive too, each blinking on a beat of its own while the world runs.
  Since [#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174) the surface pattern's windows light with the windows, and pools of light lie on the ground under the lights of a Zone built to the Finish: decals, still no light source.
  Since [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) a shop's signs, awnings and arcade glow in their own colours on the lamps' bulb, and a street light on every third pole of a finished Tile leaves its pool on the road.
  Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) every Tile's lamps and street lights leave their pools, and a taxi's roof lamp lights with the cars' head and tail lights, on the same unlit mesh, with no light source.
  Since [#177](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/177) an office tower's floors light through the pattern's lit mask, its lobby, canopy and name plates glow, and pools lie before its lobby.

## Reveal animation

On a Reveal the Level's pieces assemble brick by brick next to the slab already there: slab layers, road plate, Blocks, buildings (drop with a squash), trees and lamps (pop in), with dust where large pieces land.
A Tile's utility poles pop in once the Blocks near them have landed, and each span of wire grows out from its middle just after; the wires across its seams appear once it has landed.
Its moving details pop in just after its fields or its sheds, and a chimney smokes once it has landed.
Flagpoles pop in like trees.
A Rezoning leaves the poles standing.
When it finishes the pieces are merged into the static meshes, cars grow in, and confetti fires.
Decoration and labels for the new pieces appear after they land.
Nothing already on the slab disappears or is rebuilt.

## Sky and ambient life

All of this is decoration outside the sim.

- Clouds drift across and cast shadows. They fade out as the camera zooms in, so they never sit between the player and a jam.
- One or two propeller planes cross the sky on near-straight lines with a slight curve, vanish at the edge, and start a new line.
- Walkers follow the pavement centre lines and never cross a road.
  Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) they are of three kinds, one instanced mesh each, seen at Block zoom and facing the way they walk: a shopper with a bag (the shops' shopper, [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175)), a schoolchild in a yellow hat with a satchel, a little smaller and slower, and an office worker in a dark suit with a briefcase.
  Boats run the river.
- Labels are DOM overlays: romanised street names rotated along their Road with a white halo, and landmark names with a coloured dot.
  Japanese script appears only in speech bubbles and decoration.

## Budget

60 frames per second with 1,000 cars on an M4 Pro with the day cycle running.
The prototype measures 9.9 ms per frame with 1,000 cars at 2× pixel density, about 1.06 M triangles.
If shadows break the budget, the shadow map shrinks before any look decision changes.
Coloured light, poles and petals ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)) cost no frame time the bench can see: 7.82 ms against `main`'s 7.57 on the twelve-Tile City and 8.68 against 8.58 on 26 Tiles, inside the noise, for two or three more draw calls and under 0.1 M more triangles.
The Kerbside and the vehicles' Skins on every Tile ([#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176)), against `origin/main` (`876b1a6`, with [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) in) over three rounds on the 26-Tile City at Stage 3 with 1,000 cars, after the review's fixes, cost shops nothing the bench can see at City zoom (8.08 ms against 7.70 at noon, a gap its rounds do not hold, +0.38, −0.10 and +0.03 ms one by one, and 7.60 against 7.61 at night), now that its rails, mirrors, planters, bollards and benches are hidden there with the other Block-zoom props (0.16 M fewer triangles), and 0.21 ms at Tile zoom at night (6.03 against 5.82);
an unzoned City, the cheapest, 0.09 to 0.18 ms at City zoom (5.65 and 5.74 against 5.55 and 5.56) and 0.19 to 0.20 ms at Tile zoom (4.61 and 4.63 against 4.40 and 4.44);
and parks, the heaviest Zone, 0.08 to 0.20 ms at City zoom (10.52 and 10.63 against 10.33 and 10.54) and 0.15 to 0.17 ms at Tile zoom (6.74 and 6.75 against 6.59 and 6.59).
That bench ran with the machine about a fifth faster than the first, before the review, which read the same costs within its noise: shops 9.76 and 9.68 ms at City zoom against 9.88 and 9.87, an unzoned City 7.28 and 7.40 against 7.08, and parks 13.11 and 13.20 against 12.85 and 12.92.
City zoom gains 10 to 15 draw calls: the five skins' bodies and their shadows, the three walker kinds' and, at night, the skins' lights; Tile zoom gains up to 52, among them every build's Block-zoom mesh, which a Zone with no small props of its own now has for its street's furniture.
With 1,000 cars the skins' bodies draw 1.66 M triangles, shadows counted, against the one car's 1.89 M: 0.23 M fewer, the kei trucks' wheels 0.03 M of them.
Against `253bec3`, shops cost 1.41 ms at City zoom with [#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174)'s and [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175)'s finish and this ticket's together, and an unzoned City 0.55 ms.

A Rezoning's build, which lays a Tile's piece in one go, takes about 3 ms more a Tile (parks 21.6 ms against 18, farms 14.6 against 12, low-density 20 against 17 to 18): about 1.5 ms reading what stands near each Block's edge and 1 to 2 ms laying its street; a first cut, reading every triangle point by point over its whole face, took 5 to 9 ms.

Each Zone's own frame time is in its file (Block fill, above).

## Verification

Visuals are verified by running the game, not by tests.
Each issue is done when its part matches the reference screenshots and the prototype side by side, and the frame budget still holds with 1,000 cars.
Since [#175](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/175) `npm run shots` shows every Zone at the three zooms of [#173](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/173) (City, Tile and Block zoom) from four quarter-turns of the camera, at noon, dusk and night, a sheet per time and zoom, and `npm run bench:visual` takes `--views city,tile` and `--times noon,night`;
both take `--query` for a dev switch such as `deeper`.
The shops sheet is the reference every later Zone's sheet is held to.
Since [#176](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/176) both take `unzoned` for a City of unzoned Tiles.
Offices' sheets since [#177](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/177) are shot with `--run 45`, about noon of the working day, so that the food trucks stand at their tables.
Medium-density's since [#178](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/178) are shot with `--run 30`, so that the scooters are about and the washing hangs out, and low-density's since [#179](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/179), so that the carp fly and the cats walk.

Since [#191](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/191) `npm run check:scene` asserts the rules of Finish that are facts about what was placed, in Node, without drawing, in a few seconds a Zone: everything stands on its Block and keeps clear of parking and taxi bays, door steps and the rest, movers' paths too;
vehicles stand on their wheels;
lots side by side differ in form (rule 10);
each Block has its scenes, each with a piece longer than a car or taller than 3 m (rule 5);
something moves on every Tile (rule 6);
lights near the ground leave pools (rule 7);
small props are Block-zoom props and hide at City zoom;
no colour nears the reserved two;
triangles and meshes stay under each Zone's ceiling;
and a lot is the same lot at every Stage.
Since [#198](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/198) it reads each building's walls and roofs from their faces (`scripts/visual/scene/faces.ts`): no stretch of wall over 4 m wide and a storey tall without surface pattern, an opening or something within 0.8 m before it (rule 1), and no roof of 10 m² or more in sight without surface pattern or something standing inside its edge short of a storey, so a parapet or a ridge alone leaves a roof blank (rule 2), at every Stage.
`--seeds` sweeps the Blocks' fill, and `--against` prints each Zone's building forms and scenes and whether its meshes match the ref.
Whether walls and roofs look finished, all four sides (4), Stage 1 (8) and a Stage read at City zoom (9) still need the sheet, which is for the look alone.
What main breaks is listed with its owner in `scripts/visual/scene/known.ts`.
