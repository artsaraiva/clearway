## Goal

Replace the throwaway `src/render/scene.ts` with a renderer that draws a District as an anime-flavoured miniature diorama: a small Japanese-style town on a floating slab, readable as a traffic puzzle first. Vocabulary in root `GLOSSARY.md`.

The target is the user's "Anime Map Diorama" design. Its proof on the real sim was the prototype at `/diorama.html` (`src/lookdev/diorama.ts`, deleted from `main` in #32), kept with the three rejected looks on the local branch `16-visual-style-prototype`. Its screenshots, `.scratch/game/reference/diorama-*.png`, show the original design, whose tone the palette keeps.

Reference screenshots of the game, retaken in [#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148): `.scratch/visual-style/reference/morning.png`, `noon.png`, `afternoon.png`, `dusk.png`, `night.png`, a Jam up close in `close-noon.png`, `close-dusk.png` and `close-night.png`, and a Rebuild half-way, a new Tile's piece of slab assembling, in `rebuild.png`. They show the #104 mock's twelve Tiles, laid one cell south-west of where the mock lays them, in a Free run (`?free&seed=3`), with its two Changes to Priority rules and its west Crossroads jammed. Each Tile is a Zone: the starting Crossroads shops, then low-, medium- and high-density residential, offices, industrial, parks, green and farms in the order they are laid, and round again.

This overturns three look-dev (03) decisions: the perspective camera, static AO in place of shadows, and the baked distance-field ground.

## The rule

Readability beats style. Roads, markings, cars and jam feedback must stay as legible at night, zoomed out and mid-Reveal as they are at noon. Any effect that softens them is cut before any gameplay signal is.

## Rendering recipe

- Three.js on WebGL, standard soft-lit materials (roughness about 0.85), neutral tone mapping.
- One directional light with a 4096² shadow map, plus a hemisphere light. A shadow's edge is blurred by one texel, so it is crisp ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)). Blurred decals under every building and tree give the contact shadow, in violet (`rgb(52, 44, 150)`) like the shadows the light casts.
- No cel bands, no outlines, no ink, no painterly filter. The only post-processing allowed was one bloom pass for night lights, and only if it cost under 2 ms per frame. It was measured in [#29](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/29) at 6.2 ms and cut, so there is no post-processing.
- Everything is procedural geometry and canvas-drawn textures. No authored 3D models, no image files.
- Static scenery is merged into one mesh per material. Cars, walkers and the moving details are instanced.
- **Surface pattern** ([#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174), `src/render/pattern.ts`): the soft-lit material that walls, roofs and the slab share lays a pattern over its vertex colours from one array texture of layers 128 texels square, drawn on canvas and mipmapped. A layer's red shades the paint, its green is window glass and its blue a pale trim. A part wears a pattern through two vertex attributes, `pattern` (which, and for a wall its bays and storeys or for a roof its size) and its `uv` (where on it each vertex lies), set as the kit adds a box: each face of the box wears the pattern its side calls for, in whole bays across its width so that no window is cut at a corner. A pattern costs no triangles and no mesh; a build in which nothing wears one carries no `pattern` attribute. The glass takes one of the kit's glass tints by day, picked window by window, and 70% of the windows light at night with the kit's own windows' glow: the lit mask, which lights back and end walls without a mesh.
- **Light pools** ([#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174), `src/render/movers.ts`): warm decals on the ground under the lights, with no light source, as the contact shadows are decals. One instanced mesh for the whole City, each pool riding the brick of the light it lies under and lit as the lamps are, hidden by day. A pool adds its light to the ground and takes the place of some of the moonlight there (60% at its middle, fading to its edge as its light does): added alone to the bright blue night, a warm light comes out white.

The style comes from shape, palette and proportion:

- **Shape:** every box is rounded and bevelled; houses are a wall box with a glass band under a gable roof; office towers are curtain walls of glass with no band per floor; trees are clusters of spheres (round, sakura) or stacked cones (pine).
- **Palette:** gently oversaturated, with pastel walls and towers, brick / teal slate / teal / tan / violet / persimmon roofs, fresh greens, pink sakura, a pale grey road and an off-white pavement. Every colour is under Palette below.
- **Proportion:** lanes about 7 m wide and Blocks roughly 40–60 m across, so a car reads as a toy and each building as an object. This was the single biggest difference between the first port (a 360 m roundabout District, rejected as "not the same") and the accepted one.

## Palette

Set in [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105) at the strength the user picked in [#104](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/104), "a little", the gentlest of three. The user's reference for the tone is the original Anime Map Diorama design, which is the earlier palette under the morning light. Saturation goes into each colour: there is no filter and no post-processing. The road plate (`#c3c7ce`), the pavement (`#f1ede5`) and the white road paint stay pale and near-neutral, so cars, paint and Tool aids read against them.

**Reserved colours.** Two colours belong to the game's signals:

- Tool blue `#1f7aff`: the Tools in the world and the route Inspector. Since [#107](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/107) that covers the halo around a hovered or selected Junction, the Connections and the tube being drawn, the round markers at each Arm's line, and every lane end. The lane ends are circles where Connections start and rings where they end, both on white rims, which lift the blue off the moonlit road plate at night.
- Hot red-orange `#ff4a1f`: the anger marks, the speech bubbles' text, and the Tile in the hand when its drop is refused. That Tile takes 60% of the colour, and the tint glows (emissive 0.4) so it keeps its colour at dusk and at night. Since [#110](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/110) it also fills the Jam markers: a disc on a white rim over each Junction with a Jam, carrying the anger mark in white, with a tail that points at the Junction.

Within 30° of hue (OKLCH) of either colour, no scenery or car goes over 65% of that colour's chroma. That rule is why the park's deck is wood rather than shrine vermilion, why the cars are turquoise and raspberry rather than blue and coral, and why the roofs are brick, teal slate and persimmon rather than terracotta, slate and salmon.

A clear cyan noon, an orange and pink dusk, and a deep blue night that stays bright. The day blends between these and the morning, the late afternoon and the handovers between sun and moon, whose values are in `day.ts`.

Since [#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148) the light is coloured. A shadow shows the fill alone, so the fill's colour is the shadow's: the [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105) fill moved 70% of the way to a blue-violet at every time of day (half way at noon), and its ground bounce 30%, so walls stay warm. The sun is about 15–50% stronger and warmer in the morning, the afternoon and at dusk. Two rules set the values:

- **No road in shadow is darker than one in [#105](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/105)'s dusk shadow**, the darkest the game had and still read. Dusk's fill is raised until its shadow matches that, and noon's and the afternoon's are lowered to it, which gives the navy shadows by day. The handovers stay brighter than dusk.
- **Whites in the sun stay white at noon:** a white surface lit by noon's sun and fill is neutral within 1% on each channel. That is why noon's fill moves only half way to the blue-violet and noon's sun is not warmed.

## Camera

Orthographic. Left drag pans, right drag rotates and the wheel zooms toward the pointer, as set by [#15](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/15) and built in [#109](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/109); the camera buttons are in `.scratch/ui-ux/spec.md`. Tilt is clamped between roughly 24° and 72° above the horizon. Default view looks from the south-east at about 41° tilt with the whole slab in frame.

## The slab

- A District is drawn as a finite slab floating in open sky: soil, sand and grass layers, then a road-coloured plate. A soft shadow sits in the air beneath it. The sky is a three-stop gradient behind a transparent canvas.
- **Fog has no visual.** Unrevealed parts of a District are simply not there. Fog stays in the glossary as the rule that fogged Junctions do not exist in the sim.
- Each Level authors one or more rectangles in the District file: the pieces of slab its Reveal adds (harder Levels can add more). The slab is every piece Revealed so far.
- A Reveal only ever adds slab, next to the slab already there. A Level's Blocks are cut within its own pieces and keep clear of their outline, so a seam between two Levels' pieces is road plate and no later Reveal changes an earlier Level's Blocks. A Level's new Roads stay on its own pieces. (Settled with the user after the first Reveal playtest, 2026-11-14.)
- Roads run to the slab edge and stop. Cars grow in at a Gateway and shrink out at one. No tunnels, ramps or bridges off the slab.

## Roads and Blocks

- The road is the plate. A **Block** is a raised slab (about 1.2 m, bevelled) standing on it, coloured as pavement.
- Block outlines are cut from the lane geometry, not authored: a distance field over Road lanes at full width, the mouth of each Junction, and a car's width along every turning Connection (which rounds the corners), contoured with marching squares. The same field gives pavement centre lines for walkers and inset outlines for lawns. This handles a roundabout and a street grid with the same code. The ground bake from look-dev is retired, and its kerb-wave artefact with it.
- The prototype computes the field by brute force (1.3 s for nine Junctions). The real renderer needs a spatial grid over lane segments, and only computes the newly Revealed pieces.
- First version edges: the pavement is the rim of the Block, and a roundabout island is a raised slab with a tree. Bike bands, medians and splitter islands are cut. A Block under 12 m wide, such as the strip a seam leaves beside a Road, has no room for lots and carries a bike lane instead. The `bikeBand`, `sidewalk` and `median` Road attributes stay unread.
- **Paint:** a dashed centre line per Road, lane dividers where a direction has more than one lane, and zebra stripes at Junction mouths (decoration until pedestrians become a mechanic).
- **Priority** is shown in the world as road paint (give-way triangles on a yielding Arm) and is re-read from the sim on every Change. No in-world signs. Signal lamps are designed when the Signal Tool ([#7](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/7)) passes its gate test.

- **Moving details** ([#149](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/149), `src/render/movers.ts`), the tractors first, are drawn instanced, one mesh for every mover of a kind, and placed every frame, outside the merged static scenery:
  - A mover works a loop that its Block's fill lays out, a stretch at a time: for a tractor, a stretch is a row. It sets out at the start of the working day, from 0.02 to 0.5 of the day's clock, works as many stretches as fit in 80% of the day, going round its loop as often as that takes, and stops at the end of its last one, a tractor a metre short of the row's end, until the next morning. Where it is follows from the work done, a clock that runs only through the working day and while the world runs, so it stops while the game is paused and carries on from where it was when a Swap, a Rezoning or a Stage-up builds it again.
  - Since [#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150) a mover may wait at each stop before it sets out on the next stretch, drive a stretch in reverse, facing the way it came, as a truck backs into its bay and a forklift out of a door, and take a paint of its own, which its model's white parts show.
  - A chimney's smoke ([#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150)) rides the chimney's brick and goes with it in a Stage-up. Its puffs are one instanced mesh of soft spheres, each faded by its own share of the opacity.
  - Since [#152](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/152) a mover may fly, rising off its path between stops and easing down onto the next, and beat its wings, which turn about its body in the vertex shader and cast no shadow. Each mover's bob and wingbeat keep a phase of their own, from where its loop starts, so none jumps when movers elsewhere come or go.
  - Since [#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155) things may hang and sway in the wind, laundry on a pole and futons over a railing, each riding its building's brick as a chimney's smoke does: one instanced mesh per kind, swung about the line it hangs from, washing about 0.2 rad either way and futons 0.04, leaning out on the wind and gusting, each on its own phase. Each is hung out at its own moment early in the working day and taken in at its own moment late in it, so none hangs out at night, and it stays still while the game is paused. They cast no shadow, a thousand at a time. Beacons ride their building's brick too, one instanced mesh of bulbs, each flashing on its own beat.
  - Since [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154) a mover may work a wall, its loop drawn on the wall's face and the mover facing out of it, as a gondola does; hang from an arm by two ropes that pay out as it goes down; and work whole laps of its loop a day, so that it ends each day at the stop it set out from.
  - Flags ([#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)), and any other kind of thing that flies from a pole, are one instanced mesh per kind for the whole City, each in a colour of its own and riding its pole's brick, so that no build gains a mesh for them. Each flies downwind, on the wind that leans the chimneys' smoke, with a wave running out along it from the pole in the vertex shader, on a phase of its own; its shadow flutters with it. It is raised as the working day starts and lowered to 3 m and furled as it ends. The waves stop while the world is paused. A nobori banner ([#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)) is a kind of its own, a tall narrow cloth hung from a rod across its pole's top: it faces the way its rod is turned, not downwind, stays at the top of its pole as it is furled, and its band of lettering keeps its own colour, as a swaying thing's unpainted parts do.
  - Since [#157](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/157) a swaying thing may stretch its model along its line and down from it. The low-density gardens' laundry is a kind of its own, `cloth`: one piece pegged to a pole, a metre square stretched to the piece's width and drop, swinging as the towers' washing does, so that each piece on a pole is an instance in its own size and colour.
  - Each mover rides a brick of its own, which has no shapes. It pops in with the Rebuild, Rezoning or Stage-up that brings it, once the fields it works have landed, leaves at once with a Tile an Undo takes off, and stands until the old Tile's road plate is covered in a Swap. In a Stage-up a mover both Stages have stays where it is.
- **Every Zone's own details** ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)): each Tile type carries small details of its own, and some of them move. Each Zone's are in a ticket of their own, with the rules they share: they grow with the Stage, ignore Jams, follow the time of day, and are drawn a little oversized where they move or matter, while scatter on the ground stays true to size. Farms went first ([#149](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/149), above), since their tractors build the machinery for every moving detail; then industrial ([#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150), above), green ([#152](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/152), above), high-density residential ([#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155), above), medium-density residential ([#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156), above), offices ([#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154), above), parks ([#151](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/151)), shops ([#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)), low-density residential ([#157](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/157)) and a wilder verge on unzoned Tiles ([#158](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/158)).
- Districts are themed (city, countryside, harbor). The first is a city.

## Vehicles and jam feedback

- Cars only, in mixed colours, with a dark glass band. A bus skin arrives when the sim has a longer vehicle type.
- **Jam emotion is the main jam signal in the world.** A car that has been stuck for a few real seconds starts to shake and gets an anger mark; stuck longer, it steams and may pop a speech bubble. Marks go to the longest-stuck cars first. Bubble text is flavour only and never carries information.
- Clicking a car lights its remaining route as a glowing blue line above the road. Tool overlays (the Connection editor) use the same language: blue tubes and round markers.

## Day cycle

- A full day (morning, noon, dusk, night) loops on its own clock, a few minutes per day. It is cosmetic: it does not touch Demand, Levels or the sim, and the player cannot control it in the first version.
- One light is the sun by day and the moon by night; it goes dark for a moment at each handover so the jump across the sky is never seen. The hemisphere fill is raised through each handover, so roads are no darker there than at dusk.
- Night is a bright moonlit blue, not dark. Windows, lamps and car head and tail lights glow through emissive materials only; there are no per-object light sources. Beacons ([#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155), [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)) are emissive too, each blinking on a beat of its own while the world runs. Since [#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174) the surface pattern's windows light with the windows, and pools of light lie on the ground under the lights of a Zone built to the level of finish: decals, still no light source.

## Reveal animation

On a Reveal the Level's pieces assemble brick by brick next to the slab already there: slab layers, road plate, Blocks, buildings (drop with a squash), trees and lamps (pop in), with dust where large pieces land. A Tile's utility poles pop in once the Blocks near them have landed, and each span of wire grows out from its middle just after; the wires across its seams appear once it has landed. Its moving details pop in just after its fields or its sheds, and a chimney smokes once it has landed. Flagpoles pop in like trees. A Rezoning leaves the poles standing. When it finishes the pieces are merged into the static meshes, cars grow in, and confetti fires. Decoration and labels for the new pieces appear after they land. Nothing already on the slab disappears or is rebuilt.

## Sky and ambient life

All of this is decoration outside the sim.

- Clouds drift across and cast shadows. They fade out as the camera zooms in, so they never sit between the player and a jam.
- One or two propeller planes cross the sky on near-straight lines with a slight curve, vanish at the edge, and start a new line.
- Walkers follow the pavement centre lines and never cross a road. Boats run the river.
- Labels are DOM overlays: romanised street names rotated along their Road with a white halo, and landmark names with a coloured dot. Japanese script appears only in speech bubbles and decoration.

## Interface style

Frosted-glass pills and round buttons (compass, recenter, tilt toggle), as in the design. This spec fixes the style only; the controls are built under [#15](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/15).

## District file additions

The renderer needs, per District: a theme, the seed for Block fill, authored landmarks and special Blocks, street names, and one or more pieces of slab per Level. `roadDefaults.laneWidth` becomes about 7 m.

## Budget

60 frames per second with 1,000 cars on an M4 Pro with the day cycle running. The prototype measures 9.9 ms per frame with 1,000 cars at 2× pixel density, about 1.06 M triangles. If shadows break the budget, the shadow map shrinks before any look decision changes. Coloured light, poles and petals ([#148](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/148)) cost no frame time the bench can see: 7.82 ms against `main`'s 7.57 on the twelve-Tile City and 8.68 against 8.58 on 26 Tiles, inside the noise, for two or three more draw calls and under 0.1 M more triangles. Farm details ([#149](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/149)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City a farm at Stage 3, cost 0.46 ms (6.72 against `main`'s 6.26), for six more draw calls and 0.34 M more triangles. Their 106 tractors take 0.04 ms a frame to place and about 0.1 ms to draw: the rest is the greenhouses and the paddies' shoots. Industrial details ([#150](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/150)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City industrial at Stage 3, cost 0.73 ms (6.67 against `main`'s 5.93 over five rounds), for five more draw calls and 0.34 M more triangles. Its 245 trucks and forklifts take about 0.2 ms to draw and its smoke, about 240 puffs, under 0.1 ms; placing both takes 0.15 ms a frame, and the rest is the yards' containers and pallets. Green details ([#152](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/152)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City green at Stage 3, cost 0.32 ms (7.58 against `main`'s 7.26 over five rounds), for no more draw calls and 0.32 M more triangles, most of them wildflowers, with 281 butterflies. A first cut cost 53 more draw calls: cold frames in the kit's glass and square contact shadows each brought a material no green build drew before, and so a mesh to every green Tile. High-density details ([#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City high-density at Stage 3, cost 0.44 ms (7.19 against `main`'s 6.75 over five rounds), for eleven more draw calls and 0.49 M more triangles, with 1,193 lines of washing, 491 futons and 192 beacons. Three of the draw calls are the washing's, the futons' and the beacons' instanced meshes; the rest are round contact shadows, under the benches and the gardens' trees, on Tiles that drew none before. A first cut cost 0.83 M more triangles: rounded boxes under the playgrounds and the flower beds, and the washing's shadows. Medium-density details ([#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City medium-density at Stage 3, cost 1.02 ms (7.68 against `main`'s 6.66 over five rounds), for two more draw calls, the instanced washing and futons, and 0.58 M more triangles. The most are the bicycles, about 0.13 M with their shadows, and the outside stairs, 0.1 M. A first cut drew 1 M more triangles, until the wheels and AC grilles became flat discs and the vending machines plain boxes; and while its washing and futons swayed on a material of their own, merged into each Tile's scenery, they cost a draw call a Tile. Office details ([#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City offices at Stage 3, cost 0.37 ms (8.33 against `main`'s 7.96 over five rounds, with #155's and #156's details in), for six more draw calls and 0.19 M more triangles, with 50 gondolas, 303 flags and 506 beacons. A first cut cost 105 more draw calls: a cloth material for the flags and a material for the beacons each brought a mesh, and its shadow, to every office Tile. The flags now fly as one instanced mesh for the whole City, and the beacons are the high-density towers' instanced bulbs. Shop details ([#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City shops at Stage 3, cost 0.48 ms (8.26 against `main`'s 7.78 over five rounds, with [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)'s, [#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155)'s and [#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156)'s details in), for three more draw calls, two of them the banners', and 0.36 M more triangles, counting the shadow pass. A first cut, with a vending machine, bicycles and a cloth material of its own, cost 0.52 M more triangles; the banners now fly as [#154](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/154)'s flags, and the vending machines and bicycles are [#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156)'s. Low-density details ([#157](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/157)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City low-density at Stage 3, cost 0.63 ms (9.05 against `main`'s 8.41 over five rounds, with [#153](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/153)'s to [#156](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/156)'s details in), for two more draw calls, the instanced cloths and futons, and 0.47 M more triangles. A first cut swung its laundry in a shader of its own and cast its shadow, for four more draw calls and 0.6 M more triangles; the laundry now hangs on [#155](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/155)'s sways. Surface pattern and light pools ([#174](https://github.com/artsaraiva/clearway/issues/174)), every Tile of the 26-Tile City shops at Stage 3, cost 0.30 ms at noon (7.03 against `main`'s 6.73 over five rounds) for no more draw calls and 0.07 M fewer triangles: the pattern's windows take the place of the upper storeys' front windows that were shapes. Benched apart over five rounds, at noon and at night, the pattern alone costs 0.30 and 0.35 ms (7.05 and 7.09 against 6.75 and 6.74), the same by day as by night, and the pools, 1,897 decals, nothing the bench can see at noon, when they are hidden, and 0.03 ms at night for one more draw call; with both turned off the branch benches as `main` does. The pattern's cost is in carrying it, about 4 M vertices in 26 meshes with a `pattern` attribute each, and not in sampling its texture, which costs nothing the bench can see. A first cut passed the pattern in a flat varying, which ANGLE on Metal turns into a rework of every draw for GL's provoking vertex: 0.5 ms.

Visuals are verified by running the game, not by tests. Each issue is done when its part matches the reference screenshots and the prototype side by side, and the frame budget still holds with 1,000 cars.
